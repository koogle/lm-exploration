# Open models now ship their own speedups

As of November 2026, the biggest inference speedups for open-weight models no longer come from tricks applied after release. Labs build them into the checkpoint. Flagship releases arrive with four things already in place: 4-bit expert weights (MXFP4, INT4 QAT or FP4), a built-in drafter (MTP heads or a block drafter), sub-quadratic or compressed attention, and graded reasoning-effort controls. Each technique gives a modest gain on its own when measured end to end. NVFP4 W4A4 adds about **1.1–1.45x** serving throughput on Blackwell. Well-trained speculative drafters add **1.4–3.5x** at batch 1, falling to about 1x by 32–128 concurrent requests. FP8 KV cache adds **5–15%** throughput at long context. Length-penalized RL cuts **40–60%** of reasoning tokens on math at about ±2 points of accuracy. The multipliers in the tens (Jet-Nemotron's 47x, Fast-dLLM's 27.6x, TransMLA's 10.6x) are real. They come from shrinking the KV cache at very long context or from comparing against a very slow baseline, so they do not describe typical serving. Three research fronts moved most in 2026: parallel block drafters (DFlash, ChainFlow, DSpark) and cost-aware drafting harnesses (EAGLE-4), distillation-based quantization recovery (NVIDIA's QAD), and cheap conversion of pretrained transformers to linear, MLA or diffusion form. Almost every number below is self-reported by authors or vendors. Independent replications are rare, and where they exist they come in well below the headline figures.

## Labs now ship 4-bit weights, but end-to-end gains stay near 1.1–1.45x

The baseline in 2026 is FP8 (W8A8) plus 4-bit block-scaled floats. The low-precision checkpoint is now the reference release: benchmarks are reported on it, so upcasting gains nothing. gpt-oss post-trains its MoE weights, which are over 90% of its parameters, in **MXFP4 at about 4.25 bits/parameter**. That lets gpt-oss-120b run on one 80 GB GPU and gpt-oss-20b in 16 GB ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/openai/gpt-oss-120b); [Ollama](https://registry.ollama.ai/blog/gpt-oss)). Kimi K2 Thinking applies **weight-only INT4 QAT** to its MoE components during post-training and reports every benchmark at INT4 ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-K2-Thinking)). Moonshot's claim of a "lossless 2x" low-latency speedup has no independent check. DeepSeek V4 ships FP4 experts with FP8 for the remaining weights ([V4-Pro card](https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4-Pro)). Nemotron 3 Super is pre-trained in NVFP4 ([tech report](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/nemotron/files/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3-Super-Technical-Report.pdf)).

The two FP4 formats differ in ways that matter. **NVFP4** uses 16-element blocks with E4M3 scales (about 4.5 bits). **MXFP4** uses 32-element blocks with power-of-two E8M0 scales (about 4.25 bits) ([MLX docs](https://ml-explore.github.io/mlx/build/html/python/_autosummary/mlx.core.quantize.html)). Under W4A4, MXFP4 loses noticeably more accuracy. The format-aware MR-GPTQ closes most of that gap, to within 1–2% of NVFP4, and its kernels reach 2.2x end-to-end on B200 and 4x on RTX 5090 ([arXiv 2509.23202](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23202)). NVIDIA's pretraining study puts a cost on the difference: MXFP4 needs about **36% more training tokens** to match NVFP4 loss ([arXiv 2509.25149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25149)).

Measured serving results are more modest than the kernel numbers. NVIDIA ran a careful test of Qwen3.6-35B-A3B on 4x GB200 in vLLM. NVFP4 W4A4 beat BF16 in 9 of 12 shapes, at 1.12–1.30x, and ran at **0.87x of BF16 at concurrency 1**. Weight-only W4A16 was *slower* than BF16 in 10 of 12 shapes ([ModelOpt post](https://nvidia.github.io/Model-Optimizer/announcements/20260916-qwen36-w4a4-qad.html)). Unsloth's "Dynamic NVFP4" keeps sensitive layers in FP8/BF16. It claims 1.12x (Gemma 4 E2B) up to 1.45x (Gemma 4 31B), with MMLU-Pro, GPQA and AIME25 within noise of BF16. Kernel choice alone moves decode speed by up to 1.9x: forcing Marlin makes W4A4 MoE about 2.5x slower ([Unsloth](https://unsloth.ai/docs/basics/nvfp4)). The practical rule follows from these results. Weight-only 4-bit helps bandwidth-bound decode at low batch. Gains at high batch and in prefill need activation quantization on hardware with FP4/FP8 tensor cores, which Hopper does not have.

### Distilling against the BF16 model replaced plain QAT

The 2026 recipe for recovering 4-bit accuracy is to distill the quantized model against its own full-precision version, not to retrain it on labels. NVIDIA's **QAD** applies a KL loss from a BF16 teacher to an NVFP4 student. It recovers near-BF16 accuracy on Nemotron and AceReason models without the original SFT/RL data. It is more stable than QAT for models built through multi-stage SFT, RL and merging. The original model also beats a larger same-family model as teacher ([arXiv 2601.20088](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20088)). Gemma 3's QAT did the same thing earlier: about 5,000 steps against the unquantized model's probabilities, which Google says cut the Q4_0 perplexity drop by 54% ([Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Apr/19/gemma-3-qat-models/)).

The most useful 2026 result is a negative one. On Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, PTQ took 9 minutes and lost 2.6 pp on IFBench. 500 QAD iterations (about 735 GPU-hours) recovered that loss to −0.3 pp, but had side effects the scores did not show. SciCode completions grew **91% longer** at an unchanged score. Runs hitting the 131K token cap rose from 0.7% to 3.6%. The AA-Omniscience hallucination rate climbed from **41% (BF16) to 61% (QAD)** because the model abstained less often ([ModelOpt post](https://nvidia.github.io/Model-Optimizer/announcements/20260916-qwen36-w4a4-qad.html)). The same study saw a 3 pp benchmark swing at 3 repeats vanish at 8 repeats. This partly contradicts the 2025 finding that quantized reasoning models do not produce longer outputs ([Liu et al., arXiv 2504.04823](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04823)). The likely reconciliation is that PTQ preserves length while extra training can shift it. Evaluating a quantized reasoning model therefore needs output length, truncation rate and abstention alongside accuracy, over 8 or more repeats.

Liu et al. also mark where accuracy breaks. W8A8 and W4A16 are lossless on R1-distill and QwQ models. 3-bit weights or KV cache, and W4A4KV4, cost **3% or more**, worst at 1.5B and 7B. Larger quantized models beat smaller BF16 models on the accuracy-latency trade-off ([arXiv 2504.04823](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04823)). Below 4 bits, the serious options are trellis codebooks and native ternary training. QTIP reaches 3.87 Wikitext2 perplexity at 2 bits on Llama-2-70B, against 3.12 for FP16 ([arXiv 2406.11235](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11235)). BitNet's bitnet.cpp kernels run ternary models 1.37–5.07x faster on ARM CPUs ([arXiv 2410.16144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.16144)). Without those kernels BitNet gives no speedup.

### FP8 KV cache is close to free past about 7K tokens

`--kv-cache-dtype fp8` is now vLLM's default recommendation. It doubles KV capacity and cuts the per-token attention decode slope to **about 54% of BF16**. Accuracy recovery is 94–99% even with uncalibrated scales, including 97–99% on GPQA-D and AIME25 for reasoning models. It costs fixed overhead, so it only pays off past about **7K tokens on H100** (about 4K on B200). Before vLLM's 2026 two-level FP32 accumulation fix, a Hopper FA3 bug had dropped 128K needle-in-a-haystack accuracy from 91% to **13%** ([vLLM blog](https://vllm.ai/blog/fp8-kvcache)). DeepSeek V4.1-Flash goes further and stores its global KV cache in FP4 (E2M1 with an E4M3 scale per 16 channels), about 890 bytes per token ([V4.1-Flash card](https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash)).

## Speculative decoding moved to parallel drafters, and concurrency decides the payoff

EAGLE-3 is still the drafter format that the open tooling trains and that engines load: SpecForge/SpecBundle on SGLang, Red Hat's vLLM `speculators`, and NVIDIA ModelOpt. Research has moved on to drafters that propose a whole block in one pass. **DFlash** (ICML 2026) is a 5-layer block-diffusion drafter that denoises 16 tokens in a single step. It is conditioned on hidden states from 5 target layers and shares the target's frozen embedding and LM head. On Qwen3-8B at temperature 0 it reports **4.86x (τ 6.49)**, against EAGLE-3's 1.76–2.02x, and 4.2–4.6x in thinking mode ([arXiv 2602.06036](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06036)). Drafters exist for Qwen3.5 4B–35B-A3B and gpt-oss. **ChainFlow** (Nov 2026) uses one-step latent flow matching over VAE-compressed hidden states. It reaches 1.87x on Qwen3.5-4B and 2.12x on 27B at batch 1 ([arXiv 2611.08669](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08669)). **EAGLE-4** (20 Oct 2026) adds a harness that calibrates verify cost per batch size on the target GPU and grows the draft tree only while the expected gain beats the marginal cost. It reports **3.42x / 2.09x / 1.39x** at 1 / 8 / 64 concurrency on SGLang, and 2.83x / 1.72x / 1.14x on vLLM, with Qwen3.8-27B. Its licence is source-available, not OSI open source ([GitHub](https://github.com/eagle-hq/EAGLE-4); [fal deepdive](https://blog.fal.ai/eagle-4-technical-deepdive/)).

Matched comparisons cut these numbers sharply. ChainFlow retrained the baselines with equal 0.52B drafters, equal data and vLLM on B300. Under those conditions **DFlash got τ 2.35 and 1.69x, not τ 6–8**, while EAGLE-3 got τ 2.49 but only 1.35x because it drafts sequentially ([arXiv 2611.08669](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08669)). The harnesses, training budgets and generation lengths differ, so neither result settles the question. What they do show is that reported τ depends at least as much on the training budget and evaluation length as on the architecture. The cheapest consistent gain shows up in SpecForge, DFlash and FastMTP alike: **regenerate the drafter's training data with the target model**, which raised SpecBundle throughput by 5.3% on average ([SpecForge, arXiv 2603.18567](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18567)).

Native MTP heads are the zero-training option, and they decay with depth. DeepSeek-V3's second-token acceptance is 85–90%, for about 1.8x ([arXiv 2412.19437](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19437)). TensorRT-LLM gets 2.16x on DeepSeek-R1-FP4 with three MTP steps, and **2.33x with "relaxed acceptance"** applied only during thinking, at a slight accuracy cost ([TRT-LLM blog](https://nvidia.github.io/TensorRT-LLM/blogs/tech_blog/blog2_DeepSeek_R1_MTP_Implementation_and_Optimization.html)). Reusing one MTP head autoregressively drops GLM-4.5-Air's per-position acceptance from **0.92 to 0.68 to 0.38** ([arXiv 2601.11580](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11580)). The fix is FastMTP: fine-tune the shared head on self-distilled data. It averages 2.03x on MiMo-7B-RL ([arXiv 2509.18362](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18362)).

Batch size is the boundary condition every 2026 paper runs into. vLLM's systematic study finds that verification takes **42–95% of runtime**. A 21-node tree beats a chain at batch 1 but **falls below 1x on every workload by batch 64**, while chains stay above 1x. A separate draft model also adds 1.77x per-token KV cache, which lowers the maximum batch ([arXiv 2601.11580](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11580)). ChainFlow's tree goes from 1.85x to **0.45x at 128 concurrent**, while EAGLE-3 holds 1.06x ([arXiv 2611.08669](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08669)). MoE targets gain less because verifying k tokens activates more experts: Qwen3-235B-A22B gets 1.47–2.38x against 2.13–4.48x for Llama-4-Scout ([arXiv 2603.18567](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18567)). Reasoning traces, in contrast, suit speculation well. n-gram acceptance rises as a long chain of thought repeats itself, and an oracle that mixes EAGLE and n-gram proposals gets up to 2.2x more than the best fixed strategy ([arXiv 2601.11580](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11580)). Model-free SuffixDecoding reaches about 4x on SWE-Bench agent traces ([arXiv 2411.04975](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04975)). Adaptive choice of proposer and depth is the largest gap left open.

On Apple Silicon the gains are smaller and less reliable. A July 2026 consumer-hardware study found a best case of **1.61x**, and 3 of 5 configurations slowed down, partly because quantized Metal backends verify "parallel" drafts serially ([arXiv 2607.17283](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17283); figure from the abstract snippet). Single-blog measurements are more optimistic: MTP on MLX for Qwen 3.6 27B went from about 7 to 18.3 tok/s ([blog](https://vinoth12940.github.io/blog/articles/genai-20260519-local-mtp-speculative-decoding/)), and a llama.cpp draft model took Gemma 4 26B from 64 to 206 tok/s ([hiesch.eu](https://hiesch.eu/blog/llamacpp-benchmarks-speculative-decoding/)).

## Prune-and-distill, cheap conversions and diffusion trade the architecture for speed

**Pruning plus distillation** is NVIDIA's production recipe, with open checkpoints to show for it. Nemotron Nano 2 prunes a 12B base to 9B with Minitron ([arXiv 2508.14444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14444)). Puzzle's distillation-based architecture search turns Llama-3.1-70B into a 51B model with **2.17x throughput at 98.4% accuracy** on one H100 ([arXiv 2411.19146](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.19146)). LN-Super (49B) reaches 5x the throughput of Llama-3.3-70B at batch 256 ([arXiv 2505.00949](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00949)). For MoE models, Cerebras's REAP (ICLR 2026) shows that one-shot expert *pruning* beats expert *merging*. It is near-lossless on code at 50% expert removal for Qwen3-Coder-480B and Kimi-K2 ([arXiv 2510.13999](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13999)). The gain there is in memory, not compute, because active parameters per token stay the same. 2:4 sparsity recovers about 98% of quality after 13B tokens of distillation ([RedHatAI card](https://huggingface.co/RedHatAI/Sparse-Llama-3.1-8B-2of4)), but no source in this research measured a wall-clock speedup for it.

**Distillation into small dense models** is mostly a way to recover quality. The speed comes from choosing a smaller student. Qwen3's table makes the point. Starting Qwen3-8B from the same checkpoint, on-policy logit-KL distillation from a larger teacher scored **74.4 on AIME'24 at 1,800 GPU-hours**, while RL scored 67.6 at 17,920 GPU-hours ([Qwen3 report, arXiv 2505.09388](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09388)). The hidden cost is that the teacher must stay available in real time during training ([arXiv 2506.18330](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.18330)).

**Attention conversion** has become cheap enough for one person to run. RADLADS converts Qwen2.5 7B–72B to RWKV-6/7 hybrids with **350–700M tokens**, and the 72B conversion cost under $2,000 ([arXiv 2505.03005](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03005)). Llamba distills Llama-3.1-8B into Mamba-2 with 12B tokens ([arXiv 2502.14458](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14458)). Jet-Nemotron's PostNAS keeps a few full-attention layers in a frozen-MLP Qwen2.5 and reports **2,885 vs 61 tok/s** against Qwen3-1.7B at 64K context. The main finding is that its KV cache is 154 MB instead of 7,168 MB, and that **throughput tracks KV-cache size more than parameter count** ([arXiv 2508.15884](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.15884)). TransMLA converts GQA to MLA so that models run on DeepSeek kernels: 93% KV compression gives 10.6x at 8K in vLLM after about 6B recovery tokens ([arXiv 2502.07864](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07864)). DeepSeek-V3.2's sparse attention (DSA) shows a retrofit working at frontier scale. A lightning indexer, warmed up by KL to the dense attention pattern, selects the top 2,048 KV entries per query, and benchmarks stay near-identical to V3.1-Terminus ([arXiv 2512.02556](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02556)). The large multipliers all come from long context. At short context the gains shrink sharply.

**Diffusion LMs** are a credible speed path, but the open models depend on block-diffusion conversions and training-free accelerators. The closed Mercury Coder Mini reaches **1,109 tok/s on H100**, independently measured ([arXiv 2506.17298](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17298)). Fast-dLLM's approximate KV cache plus confidence-aware parallel decoding gives 2–3.6x from caching alone and up to **27.6x** on LLaDA. Even then LLaDA reaches only about 19 tok/s, so the multiplier is against a very slow baseline ([arXiv 2505.22618](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22618)). SDAR converts an AR model to block diffusion with about 50B tokens at roughly AR-2B parity, but reports no tok/s figure ([arXiv 2510.06303](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06303)). None of the sources compares an open diffusion model's throughput against a same-quality AR model served with speculative decoding, so whether diffusion beats speculative AR in open serving is still unknown. **Dynamic depth** has mostly given way to static pruning (Puzzle) and self-speculation. LayerSkip's 1.8–2.2x from 2024 is still the reference result ([arXiv 2404.16710](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16710)).

## Releases turned shorter thinking into an effort setting

The research results on reasoning length are consistent. RL that penalizes length or truncates at a budget cuts **40–60% of tokens on math at about ±2 points**, almost entirely on 1.5B–32B R1-distill models. ThinkPrune halves AIME24 length for about a 2% drop and takes QwQ-32B from 8,763 to 4,494 tokens ([arXiv 2504.01296](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01296)). L1/LCPO trains the model to follow a token budget given in the prompt and beats s1 budget forcing by up to 20 points absolute at matched budgets ([arXiv 2503.04697](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04697)). LASER-D reports 63% fewer tokens with +6.1 on AIME2024 ([arXiv 2505.15612](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15612)). Training-free early exit (DEER) cuts 19–80% of tokens across 11 models ([arXiv 2504.15895](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15895)). Kimi k1.5 introduced the "long2short" family of methods ([arXiv 2501.12599](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12599)).

Shipped models mostly expose this as a setting the user chooses rather than baking in shorter reasoning. gpt-oss trains low, medium and high levels, and the lever is large: gpt-oss-120b scores **50.4 / 80.0 / 92.5 on AIME 2025** across the three levels. On knowledge-heavy tasks low effort loses little: MMLU is 85.9 at low against 88.0 at medium ([model card](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/419b6906-9da6-406c-a19d-1bb078ac7637/oai_gpt-oss_model_card.pdf)). DeepSeek V4 offers Non-think, Think High and Think Max. V4.1-Flash offers a **continuous 1–100 effort integer** ([V4.1-Flash card](https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash)). GLM-5.3's High setting uses about a third fewer tokens than Max for 3.1 points less, on Z.ai's own unspecified benchmark ([Z.ai blog](https://z.ai/blog/glm-5.3), via a secondary roundup). Qwen3.5 dropped Qwen3's `/think` soft switches and documents no budget parameter ([Qwen3.5 card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-397B-A17B)). The token savings in practice come from routing easy tasks to low effort. None of the sources fetched say whether the labs use explicit length penalties in RL.

## Flagships combine every technique, and attention is where they differ

By late 2026 the flagship pattern is fine-grained MoE at about **3–5% active parameters**, a shared expert, lab-shipped 4-bit experts, a built-in drafter and some form of compressed long-context attention. The labs agree on everything except attention.

Four attention families coexist. DeepSeek-style sparse attention (DSA, then CSA/HCA) has been adopted by GLM-5. Delta-rule linear hybrids include Qwen's Gated DeltaNet and Kimi Linear's KDA, the latter with up to 6x decode at 1M context ([arXiv 2510.26692](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26692)). Sliding-window/global mixes are used by gpt-oss, Gemma and MiMo. Mamba-2 hybrids are used by Nemotron, where Nemotron 3 Nano claims 3.3x over a size-matched transformer MoE ([arXiv 2512.20856](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.20856)). MiniMax is the counterexample. It moved from linear attention in M1 to full GQA in M2 and to sparse attention in M3, a sign that linear attention costs quality on agentic tasks ([SemiAnalysis](https://inferencex.semianalysis.com/model/minimax-m27); [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/minimax-teases-upcoming-m3-model-with-new-sparse-attention-mechanism-and-15-6x-response-speed-boost)). V4.1-Flash's design shows where the optimization target is heading. It uses fewer active parameters in prefill than in decode, an FP4 KV cache, and recomputes sliding-window KV instead of storing it. By late 2026, **KV bytes per token and decode cost** matter more than parameter FLOPs. Specs for Kimi K3, Qwen3.6/3.8, GLM-5.3-Flash, Nemotron 3 Ultra and MiniMax M3 come only from aggregators and conflict with each other.

## What matters most for this repository

**Frozen-Qwen speculative decoding** (`exploration/latent-workspace/`). The repo's DFlash-style 79M drafter for frozen Qwen2.5-0.5B already scores 3.94 accepted new tokens out of 7 on its sealed test set, and a drafter for a Qwen3.5-2B target scored much lower (0.170 vs 0.387 on matched validation) (see `exploration/latent-workspace/README.md`). The 2026 literature addresses that gap directly in three ways. First, the third-party matched test that put DFlash at τ 2.35 instead of 6–8 indicates that training budget and response length dominate reported τ ([arXiv 2611.08669](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08669)). The repo's own data-scaling study points the same way. DFlash's recipe is a useful reference: about 800K target-regenerated responses for 6 epochs, conditioning on 5 uniformly spaced target layers, and a shared frozen embedding and LM head ([arXiv 2602.06036](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06036)). Second, regenerating the training data with the target is the most consistent cheap gain across SpecForge, DFlash and FastMTP ([arXiv 2603.18567](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18567)). Third, the right baselines to report against are EAGLE-3 trained with SpecForge or `speculators` and the public DFlash drafters for Qwen3.5. Results should be stated as served speedup at several concurrency levels, not τ alone, because trees lose to chains beyond batch 16–64 ([arXiv 2601.11580](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11580)). Qwen3.5's native MTP heads are a free baseline, and FastMTP-style head fine-tuning is a cheap competitor to beat ([arXiv 2509.18362](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18362)). For long-response and code prompts, a hybrid learned-plus-n-gram proposer has oracle headroom of up to 2.2x.

**Apple/Metal phone inference** (`inference/phone/`). FP4 tensor-core gains do not carry over to Apple GPUs. On a phone the benefit of quantization is bandwidth and memory, so weight-only formats are the relevant ones. MLX's quantizer supports affine 1–8-bit (group 32/64/128) and mxfp4 with E8M0 scales, which dequantizes with a shift ([MLX docs](https://ml-explore.github.io/mlx/build/html/python/_autosummary/mlx.core.quantize.html)). On a Mac, Q4_0 decoded about 29% faster than Q4_K_M on Gemma 4 12B (42.9 vs 33.2 tok/s), a single-user measurement ([3h4x blog](https://3h4x.github.io/tech/2026/06/08/gemma4-qat-vs-non-qat)). That makes simple group-32/64 4-bit or mxfp4 the natural packed-weight targets for handwritten kernels. NVFP4's E4M3 scales cost more to decode in software and give nothing back off Blackwell. The finding most directly relevant to this work is that speculative decoding on Apple fails mainly because of kernels. Quantized Metal backends verify the k drafted tokens serially, which is behind the 1.61x best case and the slowdowns ([arXiv 2607.17283](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17283)). A Metal kernel that truly batches a small multi-token matmul over packed weights is therefore the step that unlocks drafting on device, and short chains (MTP-1/2, n-gram) are safer than trees. For KV, 8-bit with per-head or per-token scales is the safe first step. Below 8-bit, reasoning accuracy degrades on small models ([arXiv 2504.04823](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04823)). The fixed-overhead break-even seen with vLLM's FP8 KV probably applies to Metal too, but no source measured it.

**From-scratch training** (`training/`). For 124M-class models that will run on a phone, the evidence favors building low precision and fast decode into training rather than adding them afterwards. A Gemma-style distillation-QAT pass of a few thousand steps against the BF16 model's own probabilities is cheap. QAD's finding that the original model is the best teacher supports the same recipe ([arXiv 2601.20088](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20088)). Sub-4-bit inference holds up only when it is trained in, as with BitNet ternary or FP4 pretraining. PTQ at 2 bits stays fragile ([arXiv 2410.16144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.16144); [arXiv 2509.25149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25149)). Two architecture choices from releases are cheap to adopt at small scale and cut KV bytes, which is what bounds on-device decode: sliding-window/global layer mixes and attention sinks, as in gpt-oss and Gemma. An MTP auxiliary head trained with the trunk gives a free drafter. Fine-tuning must preserve that head, because the HF Trainer drops MTP weights. For a small student, Qwen3's result favors SFT on teacher outputs followed by on-policy logit-KL over RL ([arXiv 2505.09388](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09388)). Reasoning-length RL matters only if the model learns to reason.

## Conclusion

Labs now design for inference speed from the start. By the time an open checkpoint is released, its precision, drafter, attention and effort controls are already fixed, so the remaining post-release work is mostly adapting it: retraining drafters on target-regenerated data, QAD for the hardware's FP4 format, conversion to whatever attention the serving stack has kernels for, and choosing an effort level per task. The gains also compound less than the headlines suggest. Quantization, speculation and sparse attention all cut the same bottleneck, the memory traffic of bandwidth-bound decode, and speculation's gain falls toward 1x under the high concurrency that quantization and smaller KV caches make possible. Each serving regime therefore has its own best combination.

The weakest part of the 2026 evidence is evaluation. Speedups are reported without stating concurrency, engine, temperature or generation length. Matched re-tests cut the drafter claims by 2–3x. Quantization recovery can keep scores flat while making outputs longer and models abstain less. Few public results have been checked carefully, so a small lab that reports served speedups at several concurrency levels, per-position acceptance, output length and abstention, over enough repeats, adds a lot. That applies directly to this repo's frozen-Qwen drafter and Metal kernel work.
