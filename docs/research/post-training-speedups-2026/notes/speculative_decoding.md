# Speculative Decoding and Post-hoc Multi-Token Prediction for LLM Inference (state as of 2026-11-10)

Scope: drafting methods trained after the base model exists, how open-model releases ship them, and measured acceptance lengths (τ / AL) and speedups. Unless marked otherwise, all numbers are self-reported by authors or vendors. I found very few independent replications. Source quality is labeled where it matters (primary paper or repo vs. aggregator or blog).

## 1. Post-hoc drafting methods, training tooling, and released heads (EAGLE-3 and successors, diffusion/flow drafters, MTP heads, model-free methods)

### Takeaway
The 2026 frontier has moved past EAGLE-3's autoregressive chain/tree drafter toward **parallel block drafters**: DFlash (block diffusion, ICML 2026), DFlash2, DSpark, ChainFlow (flow matching), and others. It has also moved toward **cost-aware adaptive drafting**: EAGLE-4 (Oct 2026) is one drafter plus a harness that sizes the tree from measured verify costs per batch size. EAGLE-3 is still the default "trainable head" format that open tooling (SpecForge/SpecBundle, vLLM `speculators`, NVIDIA ModelOpt) produces and that engines load. Native MTP heads shipped with models (DeepSeek-V3/R1, GLM-4.5/5, Qwen3-Next/3.5/3.6, MiMo) are the zero-training option. Their later-position acceptance degrades because a single head is reused autoregressively, and post-hoc fine-tuning of those heads (FastMTP) is the fix.

### Cited Findings

**EAGLE-3 (Mar 2025, background-but-baseline)**
- EAGLE-3 paper: up to ~6.5x over vanilla decoding, ~1.4x over EAGLE-2. Vicuna-13B MT-Bench 5.58x, HumanEval 6.47x. SGLang on H100 at batch size 1 is 158 tok/s base, 244 with EAGLE-2, 373 with EAGLE-3, and ~1.38x throughput at batch 64. Acceptance length is ~6+ for EAGLE-3 vs ~4 for EAGLE-2 with 8x training data. — [EAGLE-3 paper summary (aggregator of arXiv 2503.01840)](https://ai-paper-delta.vercel.app/en/papers/2503.01840); [aitopics](https://aitopics.org/tag/eagle-3)
- Real-world EAGLE-3 τ on modern targets is much lower than in the paper. On AMD MI325X, Kimi-K2.5-W4A8 with EAGLE-3 (3 steps, top-k 1, 4 draft tokens) gave τ ≈ 3.9–4.0. — [AMD ROCm blog](https://rocm.blogs.amd.com/artificial-intelligence/kimi-k2.5-speculative/README.html)
- ChainFlow's retrained EAGLE-3 baseline on Qwen3.5-4B (vLLM 0.31.0, B300, batch 1, K=4): τ 2.49, 1.35x served speedup. — [ChainFlow, arXiv 2611.08669](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08669)

**DFlash: block-diffusion drafter (Z-Lab, arXiv Feb 2026, ICML 2026)**
- Architecture: 5 draft layers (8 for Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B), block size 16 (10 for Llama-3.1-8B), one denoising step. It is conditioned on 5 target layers sampled uniformly from layer 2 to the 3rd-to-last, and shares the target's frozen embedding and LM head. Training used ~800K samples (Nemotron Post-Training v2 + CodeAlpaca) with responses regenerated by the target, for 6 epochs at LR 6e-4. Extra projection memory is ~42 MB for Qwen3.5-35B-A3B. — [DFlash, arXiv 2602.06036](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06036); [PMLR v306](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/chen26bv.html)
- Temperature 0, Transformers backend: Qwen3-8B with DFlash averages 4.86x (τ 6.49), vs EAGLE-3 tree-16 at 1.76x (τ 2.96) and tree-60 at 2.02x (τ 3.40). MATH-500 reaches 6.08x (τ 7.87) and MT-Bench 2.75x (τ 4.24). At temperature 1, Qwen3-8B with DFlash averages 4.03x (τ 5.48) vs EAGLE-3-60 at 1.88x. — [DFlash](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06036)
- Thinking mode, Qwen3-8B at temp 0: GPQA 4.17x (τ 5.17), MATH-500 4.64x (τ 5.82), AIME25 4.51x (τ 5.74). At temp 1 the range is 3.70–4.03x. — [DFlash](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06036)
- The appendix covers Qwen3.5-4B/9B/27B/35B-A3B, Qwen3-Coder-Next, and GPT-OSS-20B/120B targets, so DFlash drafters exist for current open models. — [DFlash](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06036)
- Independent contrast: under ChainFlow's matched setting (0.52B drafters, same data, vLLM 0.31.0, B300, batch 1, 256-token generations), DFlash had τ 2.35 and 1.69x, far below DFlash's own reported τ 6–8. EAGLE-3 had a higher τ (2.49) but lower speedup (1.35x), because DFlash's single-pass drafting is cheaper. — [ChainFlow](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08669) (the two papers use different training budgets, generation lengths and harnesses, so the numbers cannot be compared directly)

**ChainFlow: flow-matching drafter (arXiv 2611.08669, Nov 2026)**
- Method: VAE-compressed target hidden states, plus one-step latent flow matching from a linear extrapolation of recent latents, plus path/Markov heads and beam search into a chain or an 8×4 tree. Drafter sizes are 529M / 1.16B / 1.64B for Qwen3.5-4B / 9B / 27B. — [ChainFlow](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08669)
- At batch 1, the 8×4 tree gets τ 2.78 and 1.87x on 4B, i.e. 1.39x over EAGLE-3 and 1.11x over DFlash. On 27B it gets 2.12x, peaking at 3.23x on math. — [ChainFlow](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08669)
- **Concurrency collapse:** on 4B, ChainFlow tree speedup goes 1.85 / 1.12 / 0.94 / 0.81 / 0.63 / 0.45 at 1 / 4 / 8 / 16 / 64 / 128 concurrent requests. EAGLE-3 goes 1.33 → 1.06 and DFlash goes 1.66 → 1.14 over the same range. — [ChainFlow](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08669)
- 4–13% of prompts diverge from plain greedy output on B300, attributed to fp16 near-ties, so the authors do not claim bitwise equivalence. — [ChainFlow](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08669)
- Related 2026 drafters cited there, without numbers: DSpark (semi-autoregressive drafting with recurrent correction heads), DART, JetSpec (parallel trees), Domino, BASTION (budget-aware block-diffusion trees), and PARD (2025, parallel draft adaptation). DART is also credited with the claim that drafting consumes 20–40% of inference time. — [ChainFlow related work](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08669)

**EAGLE-4 (Eagle AI Corp with Fireworks/fal, released 2026-10-20)**
- Pitch: "One Flexible Drafter for All Batch Sizes and Hardware." The drafter is an EAGLE-3 descendant with sliding-window attention and a low-rank candidate head that scores the vocabulary cheaply and computes full logits only for candidates. "EAGLE-Harness" runs a one-time offline calibration on the target GPU, building a cost table of verify latency by batch, context and candidate count. It grows the tree while the cumulative-probability gain per marginal cost stays above a threshold, uses rejection sampling, and is lossless. — [fal technical deepdive](https://blog.fal.ai/eagle-4-technical-deepdive/); [GitHub eagle-hq/EAGLE-4](https://github.com/eagle-hq/EAGLE-4)
- The only released checkpoint is `EAGLE-Corp/Qwen3.8-27B-EAGLE4`. The **license is source-available, not OSI open source**. It integrates with SGLang (custom build `sglang==0.5.23.dev252+eagle.9`), vLLM, TokenSpeed and AMD MI355X/ROCm. — [GitHub](https://github.com/eagle-hq/EAGLE-4); [HF card](https://huggingface.co/EAGLE-Corp/Qwen3.8-27B-EAGLE4); [AMD day-0 article](https://www.amd.com/en/developer/resources/technical-articles/2026/eagle-4-on-amd-instinct-gpus.html)
- Qwen3.8-27B at temp 0 on 4×B300, speedup vs the same engine without speculation: SGLang 3.42x / 2.09x / 1.39x at 1 / 8 / 64 concurrency. vLLM 2.83x / 1.72x / 1.14x. TokenSpeed 3.09x / 2.02x / 1.41x. The high-load recipe "keeps pace with plain decoding up to 512 concurrent requests," which means no loss rather than a gain. On MI355X with SGLang: 2.07x at 8 concurrency, 1.56x at 32. — [GitHub](https://github.com/eagle-hq/EAGLE-4)
- AL is 2.66 on the highest-entropy prompt quartile and 2.97 on the lowest. A tree beats a chain by 9.6–12.4% in AL, and by 21–26% at verify budget 16. At TP=4 on B300, C=1 gives 660.6 tok/s, which is 1.63–1.85x over the "fastest alternative" (DFlash2/DSpark). — [fal deepdive](https://blog.fal.ai/eagle-4-technical-deepdive/)
- An aggregator cites 863.8 tok/s single-request on 27B, 1.28x over DFlash2 and 1.40x over DSpark, and notes community skepticism that these are temp-0, single-request best cases. — [daily.dev summary](https://daily.dev/posts/eagle-4-speeds-up-llm-inference-with-an-adaptive-speculative-decoding-drafter-2hx4eb8tv) (secondary source)

**Other 2026 research directions (abstract-level claims only)**
- Test-Time Speculation: up to 67% (avg ~34%) longer τ than vanilla EAGLE-3. It argues EAGLE-3, DFlash and PARD degrade on long generations. — [arXiv 2605.09329](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.09329v1)
- Speculative Speculative Decoding (Kumar, Dao, May; ICLR 2026): ~30% faster than the strongest SD baselines, including EAGLE-3. — [arXiv 2603.03251](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.03251)
- Also noted but not read: Speculative Pipeline Decoding [2605.30852](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.30852), Osprey target-agnostic drafter pretraining [2609.09338](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.09338), acceptance-aware draft training [2609.24150](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.24150), "Attention Drift: what AR speculative drafters learn" [2605.09992](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.09992), adversarial prompts for acceptance collapse [2607.21804](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.21804), and Mamba drafters [2506.01206](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.01206).

**Native MTP heads in open releases**
- DeepSeek-V3: the 2nd-token acceptance rate is 85–90% across topics, giving ~1.8x TPS. — [DeepSeek-V3 tech report](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.19437v1)
- In vLLM's early DeepSeek MTP PR, R1 with k=1 had 81–82.3% acceptance. One commenter saw <50% (later ~56%) with MLA enabled. Older vLLM sampled drafts greedily regardless of temperature, which understated acceptance at T>0. — [vLLM PR #12755](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/12755); [nebius/MTP-DeepSeek-V3-0324 card](https://huggingface.co/nebius/MTP-DeepSeek-V3-0324)
- TensorRT-LLM DeepSeek-R1-FP4 on 8×B200 at batch 1: MTP nextn=3 gives 2.16x min-latency speedup over nextn=0, and **2.33x with "relaxed acceptance"** (top-N=10, δ=0.6, applied only in the thinking phase, with a "slight accuracy drop"). "MTP Eagle", which chains the MTP module EAGLE-style, is the default path for DeepSeek-V3/R1. — [TRT-LLM tech blog 2](https://nvidia.github.io/TensorRT-LLM/blogs/tech_blog/blog2_DeepSeek_R1_MTP_Implementation_and_Optimization.html)
- GLM-5 report: with 4 speculative steps on a private prompt set, accept length is 2.76 for GLM-5 vs 2.55 for DeepSeek-V3.2. — [GLM-5, arXiv 2602.15763](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.15763)
- GLM-4.5-Air MTP in vLLM reached 1.3x–1.8x on GPQA-Main. Reusing one MTP head autoregressively makes position-wise acceptance fall 0.92 → 0.68 → 0.38. — [Speculative Decoding: Performance or Illusion?, arXiv 2601.11580](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.11580v2)
- Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B-FP8 in vLLM (AMD ATOM PR), 1 speculative token: mean AL 1.91, i.e. 0.906 acceptance (3717/4104). — [ROCm/ATOM PR #772](https://github.com/ROCm/ATOM/pull/772)
- Qwen3-Next's shipped MTP head was trained only on next-token prediction from ground-truth inputs. Its position-2 acceptance was 0.476, rising to 0.616 after FastMTP-style fine-tuning. — [daily.dev summary of FastMTP-for-vLLM write-up](https://daily.dev/posts/optimize-vllm-speculative-decoding-with-fastmtp-heads-znpaupcon) (secondary source)
- FastMTP (Sep 2025): fine-tunes a single position-shared MTP head on self-distilled data. On MiMo-7B-RL (single-layer MTP module) it averages 2.03x across 7 benchmarks, lossless. — [FastMTP, arXiv 2509.18362](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.18362)
- Practical pitfall: the HF Trainer drops MTP weights on fine-tune, so vLLM spec decoding fails. Copying the base model's MTP tensors back in "works" but leaves the heads mismatched with the fine-tuned trunk. — [community post, aggregated in FastMTP search results](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1sfsxv2/snapshots/2026-04-09T16%3A11%3A00.655487Z) (anecdotal)
- A community NVFP4 Qwen MoE with MTP and 3 speculative tokens had 69.3% acceptance and AL 3.08, with per-position acceptance falling 78% → 62%. — [friendli.ai model page](https://friendli.ai/models/lgmc/Qwen-AgentWorld-35B-A3B-NVFP4-MTP) (anecdotal)

**Training tooling**
- **SpecForge (LMSYS/SGLang, arXiv 2603.18567):** EAGLE-3 training with the target served on SGLang's engine, the draft under ZeRO-2, FlexAttention sparse tree attention, and an in-place Triton masked log-softmax backward (30–40% memory saving). Training throughput beats the prior EAGLE codebase by 2.01x (Llama-3.1-8B) up to 9.99x (Qwen3-235B-A22B) on 8×H200. — [SpecForge](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18567)
- **SpecBundle recipe:** Open-PerfectBlend (1.4M conversations), responses regenerated by the target at T=0.8, trained from scratch for 2 epochs at LR 1e-4 cosine with TTT length 7. TTT-length sweeps gain most from 1 → 3; ~3 is best for chat and ~13 for math/code. All MoE draft variants trailed the dense draft. Regenerating data raised τ nearly everywhere, for +5.3% average throughput. — [SpecForge](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18567)
- SpecBundle covers Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-3.3-70B, Llama-4-Scout/Maverick, Qwen3-30B-A3B, Qwen3-235B-A22B, Ling-Flash-V2 and Kimi-K2, with GPT-OSS and DeepSeek-V3 mentioned. — [SpecForge](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18567); [SGLang SpecBundle supported models](https://cookbook.sglang.io/specbundle/supported_models)
- **vLLM `speculators` (Red Hat):** EAGLE-3 training and format, with heads for Llama-3.1-8B/70B, Qwen3-8B/14B/32B, Qwen3-30B/235B MoE, Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B, gpt-oss-20b/120b and Gemma 4. Mistral 3 Large 675B is in progress. The RedHatAI gpt-oss-20b EAGLE-3 head has 854M params, was trained by distilling gpt-oss-120b hidden states, and needs vLLM ≥0.17.1. — [PyPI speculators](https://pypi.org/project/speculators/); [docs](https://docs.vllm.ai/projects/speculators/en/latest/reference/speculators/); [dev.co listing](https://dev.co/ai/llms/gpt-oss-20b-speculator-eagle3) (secondary source)
- **NVIDIA:** publishes four EAGLE-3 models for gpt-oss-120b. An independent RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell test got +60% (650 tok/s) on gpt-oss-120b. — [Fixstars blog](https://blog.us.fixstars.com/?p=1801). NVIDIA trained an EAGLE-3 draft for Llama 4 Maverick as part of a 4x stack-wide speedup, reaching >1,000 tok/s/user on DGX B200. The 4x is not attributable to EAGLE-3 alone. — [insideHPC, May 2025](https://insidehpc.com/2025/05/ai-inference-nvidia-reports-blackwell-surpasses-1000-tps-user-barrier-with-llama-4-maverick/)
- **Community HF heads** exist for Qwen3.5-27B-FP8 EAGLE-3 and Qwen3.5 MoE. One card's rule of thumb is that mean AL > 2.0 is needed for a useful speedup. — [VirVen/Qwen3.5-27B-EAGLE3-v2](https://huggingface.co/VirVen/Qwen3.5-27B-EAGLE3-v2); [Ichigec a1-agents EAGLE3](https://huggingface.co/Ichigec/a1-agents-eagle3-speculator)
- One 2026 paper notes it could not find official EAGLE-3 heads for Qwen3 on HF and used Tencent AngelSlim releases instead. — [arXiv 2605.09329](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.09329)

**Model-free: n-gram and suffix decoding**
- SuffixDecoding (Snowflake Arctic Inference, a vLLM plugin): per-request plus global suffix trees. On OpenHands SWE-Bench with 4×H100 it gets 1.8–4.5x, with ~4x end-to-end for agents and up to 2.8x on open-ended workloads. It can be combined with Arctic's LSTM speculator. It beats vLLM n-gram at low concurrency but falls behind at concurrency 64. — [SuffixDecoding arXiv 2411.04975](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.04975); [Snowflake blog](https://www.snowflake.com/en/engineering-blog/suffixdecoding-arctic-inference-vllm/); [Arctic docs](https://arcticinference.readthedocs.io/en/latest/suffix-decoding.html)
- On InstructCoder (code editing), when prompt-output BLEU-4 > ~0.6, n-gram beats EAGLE by up to 53% with 3 tokens and 100% with 5. Generated length per step on InstructCoder: n-gram 7.27, EAGLE-3 4.18. — [arXiv 2601.11580](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.11580v2)

### Inferences
- For the user's frozen-Qwen research, the strongest current comparison baselines are DFlash (block diffusion, Qwen3/3.5 drafters available) and EAGLE-3 trained with SpecForge or `speculators`. EAGLE-4 is relevant mostly for its cost-model harness, and its license limits reuse. DFlash's τ 6–8 claims are in its own Transformers/SGLang harness. Under a matched third-party setup, its τ was ~2.35, which suggests training budget and generation length dominate reported τ.
- Native MTP heads are good at position 1 (~0.8–0.9) but decay sharply by position 3 (~0.4). Post-hoc fine-tuning of the MTP head on self-distilled data (FastMTP) is the cheap upgrade path, and any SFT of the trunk should also re-train or at least preserve the MTP head.
- Regenerating training data with the target (self-distillation) is a consistent, low-cost τ gain across SpecForge, DFlash and FastMTP.

### Gaps
- I did not re-verify the following in this session; they are background (2023–early 2025) and their arXiv IDs come from prior knowledge: Medusa (2401.10774), Hydra (2402.05109), HASS (2408.15766), GRIFFIN (2502.11018), DistillSpec (2310.08461), LayerSkip (2404.16710), SWIFT (2410.06916), Lookahead decoding (2402.02057). I have no fresh 2026 measurements for them, and none appears as a headline baseline in 2026 papers, where EAGLE-3, DFlash, DSpark and PARD dominate.
- No primary numbers on Xiaomi MiMo's own MTP acceptance, Qwen3.5/3.6 official MTP acceptance, or Llama 4 MTP.
- No primary papers found for DFlash2 or DSpark. They are known only through ChainFlow and EAGLE-4 comparisons.
- The SpecForge paper's Llama-4-Scout "Existing" row duplicates numbers from another row, possibly a copy error, so treat that comparison cautiously.

## 2. Speculative decoding for reasoning models and at large batch sizes: when it stops helping

### Takeaway
Gains shrink with batch size and model size, because verification dominates runtime (42–95%). Wide trees are the first casualty: they fall below 1x by ~batch 16–64, while chains and cheap parallel drafters (DFlash) keep 1.1–3x up to batch 32–128. Reasoning (thinking) traces are speculation-friendly: DFlash keeps ~4–4.6x in thinking mode, and EAGLE-3 gets 1.6–1.8x on Qwen3-8B-Thinking. Relaxed (lossy) acceptance during the thinking phase is a production trick in TRT-LLM. Adaptive per-batch tree sizing (EAGLE-4 harness, oracle studies) is the 2026 answer to "when to turn it off."

### Cited Findings
- vLLM systematic study (v0.10.1.1, H100): Llama3.1-8B EAGLE on GSM8K falls from 1.73x at batch 1 to 1.21x at batch 128. A 21-node tree helps at batch 1 (1.85x vs 1.65x chain on Qwen3-8B GSM8K) but **falls below 1x on all workloads at batch 64**, while the chain stays above 1x. — [arXiv 2601.11580](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.11580v2)
- Same study: verification takes 42–95% of time. Drafting share for EAGLE/EAGLE-3 is 12–20% at batch 1 and 3–7% at batch 512. A Qwen3-0.6B draft model takes 47% of time at batch 1 for Qwen3-8B. A separate draft model adds 1.77x per-token KV cache (144 → 256 KiB), which reduces maximum batch. — [arXiv 2601.11580](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.11580v2)
- Same study, reasoning: Qwen3-8B-Thinking with EAGLE-3 gets 1.64–1.80x on GPQA-Main and AIME22-24 (32K outputs), and n-gram gets 1.50–1.58x. n-gram acceptance rises in long reasoning outputs (as the model repeats itself) and drops near the final answer. An oracle mixing EAGLE and n-gram gives up to 2.2x more speedup than the best fixed strategy. — [arXiv 2601.11580](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.11580v2)
- DFlash on SGLang with one B200, Qwen3-8B Math500: 5.1x / 4.5x / 4.5x / 3.9x / 2.8x at concurrency 1 / 4 / 8 / 16 / 32. On vLLM with Qwen3.5-9B, MT-Bench drops from 3.0x at c=1 to 1.3x at c=32. EAGLE-3 with tree 60 drops below 1x at concurrency 16–32 on several tasks. — [DFlash](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06036)
- EAGLE-3 retains 1.20x at 64 concurrent and 1.06x at 128, while ChainFlow's 32-node tree drops to 0.45x at 128. — [ChainFlow](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08669)
- EAGLE-4 on SGLang/B300 goes from 3.42x at c=1 to 1.39x at c=64, and is "keeps pace" (≈1x) up to 512 concurrent with a high-load recipe. — [EAGLE-4 GitHub](https://github.com/eagle-hq/EAGLE-4)
- AgentSpec: on Qwen-3-8B agent workloads, SD became slower than AR decoding once max batch exceeded 32. — [AgentSpec, arXiv 2608.24004](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.24004)
- Practitioner guidance says the benefit fades at batch ≥32, and one vendor guide claims a 30–40% throughput loss at 100+ concurrent. — [Red Hat Developers, Jun 2026](https://developers.redhat.com/articles/2026/06/12/how-speculative-decoding-delivers-faster-llm-inference); [Spheron guide](https://www.spheron.network/blog/speculative-decoding-production-guide/) (vendor source, weaker evidence)
- SparseSpec (self-speculation with sparse attention for large reasoning models): up to 2.13x throughput, with a unified scheduler batching draft and verify. — [arXiv 2512.01278](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01278)
- TRT-LLM relaxed acceptance for DeepSeek-R1, applied only in the thinking phase: 2.16x → 2.33x at batch 1, with a "slight" accuracy drop. — [TRT-LLM blog](https://nvidia.github.io/TensorRT-LLM/blogs/tech_blog/blog2_DeepSeek_R1_MTP_Implementation_and_Optimization.html)
- Test-Time Speculation argues EAGLE-3, DFlash and PARD degrade on long generations. — [arXiv 2605.09329](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.09329v1)
- In the SpecForge/SpecBundle evaluation at 8–16 concurrent requests, MoE targets gain less than dense ones: Qwen3-235B-A22B gets 1.47–2.38x vs Llama-4-Scout at 2.13–4.48x, and Kimi-K2 gets 1.24–2.08x. — [SpecForge](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18567)
- SuffixDecoding falls behind vLLM n-gram at concurrency 64. — [Snowflake](https://www.snowflake.com/en/engineering-blog/suffixdecoding-arctic-inference-vllm/)

### Inferences
- Rough 2026 crossover rules: wide trees stop helping by ~16–64 concurrent, chains of 3–4 tokens survive to ~64–128, and beyond that SD is ≈1x at best unless the drafter cost is tiny and depth is chosen adaptively. MoE targets gain less because verifying k tokens activates more experts, so verify cost grows with k.
- For long CoT, n-gram/suffix acceptance grows over a trace while learned-drafter acceptance may degrade with length. Hybrid proposers (learned + n-gram) are an under-exploited lever, per the oracle results.
- Measured speedups remain well below oracle bounds, so adaptive length and method selection is the main open opportunity.

### Gaps
- No primary source reports acceptance per position across 32K+ reasoning traces at large batch for EAGLE-3 or MTP.
- No independent large-batch benchmark of EAGLE-4 or DFlash2.
- AgentSpec and SparseSpec were read only at abstract or snippet level.

## 3. Measured speedups in vLLM, SGLang and TensorRT-LLM, and on-device (llama.cpp, MLX, Apple)

### Takeaway
Datacenter batch-1 speedups for well-trained post-hoc drafters on current open models are typically 1.4–3.5x in served engines: EAGLE-3 ~1.3–2.5x, DFlash ~3–5x on math/code, EAGLE-4 2.8–3.4x, and DeepSeek MTP ~2.2x. The same method varies by engine (vLLM vs SGLang differ by up to ~20%). On Apple Silicon, gains are smaller and less reliable (1.5–1.8x typical, best 3x on a blog). The causes are memory-bound quantized kernels and Metal backends that verify "parallel" drafts serially.

### Cited Findings
- **SGLang, H200, SpecBundle EAGLE-3 at 8–16 concurrency:** Llama-3.1-8B 1.39x (FinanceQA) to 3.34x (Math500). Llama-4-Scout up to 4.48x (LiveCodeBench). Qwen3-235B-A22B 1.47–2.38x. Kimi-K2 1.24–2.08x. SpecBundle beats prior public EAGLE-3 heads by up to ~1.35x, e.g. Qwen3-235B Math500 1.39x → 2.38x. — [SpecForge](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18567)

### Inferences
- On-device, verify cost per extra token is not ~free the way it is on a GPU at batch 1, so cheap drafters with short chains (MTP-1/2, n-gram) are safer than trees. Verify kernels that truly batch the k drafted tokens (multi-token matmul on quantized weights) are the bottleneck. This matters for the user's handwritten-Metal work: speedups from SD on Apple are a kernel problem as much as a drafter problem.
- Reported "Nx" figures need the engine, concurrency, temperature and generation length attached. The same drafter shifts by 20%+ across engines and by 2–3x between batch 1 and batch 64.
